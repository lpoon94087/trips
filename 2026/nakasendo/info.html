Arriving in Japan

No visa needed for US, Canadian or Hong Kong (HKSAR or BN(O)) passports for a stay this short.

  • Each traveler shows their own Visit Japan Web QR code at immigration and customs. Keep a screenshot in Photos.
  • If you edit anything in Visit Japan Web, take a new screenshot; the code changes.
  • At Kansai T1, the limousine bus counter is in arrivals. Bus stop 5, about ¥1,800.

Cash and cards
  • Apple Pay and contactless cards work in cities, stations, convenience stores and most hotels.
  • Bring cash for the Kiso Valley and Kusatsu: rural trains, the Magome bus, the Tsumago bento, small shops.
  • 7-Eleven ATMs take foreign cards and have English menus.
  • If a terminal asks for a PIN on a card without one, ask for signature.
  • Carry a second card on a different network in case one is declined.
Weather and packing

Warm in Osaka, cold mornings in the mountains, mild in Tokyo. The Today card shows a live forecast during the trip.

  • A battery pack for hiking days; GPS drains phones fast.

Birding

The Japan pack is downloaded in Merlin, so Sound ID works offline.

Forest residents to listen for in Karuizawa and the Kiso Valley in October: varied tit, Japanese tit, long-tailed tit, Eurasian nuthatch, Japanese pygmy woodpecker, brown-eared bulbul, Eurasian jay.

Picchio also runs evening tours to watch giant flying squirrels.

Onsen manners
  • Wash and rinse fully at the shower stools before getting in.
  • No swimsuits. The small towel stays out of the water; fold it on your head or the edge.
  • Tie long hair up.
  • Kusatsu water is very hot and acidic: keep it out of your eyes, take silver jewelry off (it tarnishes), and drink water between soaks.
  • Free local baths (Shirahata, Jizō) have no showers. Bring your own towel.
  • Tattoo rules vary by bath; ask at the front desk if it matters.
